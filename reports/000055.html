What to watch for

Filter settings: the data and the paper disagree.

The files say 250 Hz lowpass; the paper says: Shared ECoG: median DC removal, 1–200 Hz band-pass, 60 Hz and harmonics notch, downsampled to 500 Hz. You can't tell which frequencies are real signal and which were removed, so spectral features may not be comparable across sites.

“With data discontinuities removed, we then band-pass ﬁltered the data (1–200 Hz), notch ﬁltered to minimize line noise at 60 Hz and its harmonics, downsampled to 500 Hz, and re-referenced to the common median for each grid, strip, or depth electrode group.” · 10.1101/2021.07.26.453884
Recording system / amplifier: missing.

Neither the data nor an open-access paper states it. You can't correct for hardware differences when combining this with data from other hospitals.

Consent scope: missing.

Neither the data nor an open-access paper states it. It's unclear whether reuse, especially commercial reuse, is covered by what patients agreed to.

Reference scheme: only in the paper.

The paper says: Shared data re-referenced to the common median of each grid, strip, or depth electrode group. The data doesn't state it, so treat this as unconfirmed. The same activity looks different under different references, so signals may not be comparable to other datasets.

“With data discontinuities removed, we then band-pass ﬁltered the data (1–200 Hz), notch ﬁltered to minimize line noise at 60 Hz and its harmonics, downsampled to 500 Hz, and re-referenced to the common median for each grid, strip, or depth electrode group.” · 10.1101/2021.07.26.453884
Electrode localization method: only in the paper.

The paper says: Fieldtrip; preop MRI coregistered with postop CT, manual electrode selection, warped to MNI space. The data doesn't state it, so treat this as unconfirmed. You can't place contacts reliably in a shared brain space, so cross-patient analysis is limited.

“This process in- volved coregistering preoperative MRI and postoperative computed tomography scans, manually selecting electro- des in 3D space, and warping electrode positions into Montreal Neurological Institute (MNI) space.” · 10.1523/eneuro.0007-21.2021
Subjects by diagnosis: only in the paper.

The paper says: 12 subjects (8 male, 4 female) undergoing clinical epilepsy monitoring. The data doesn't state it, so treat this as unconfirmed. You can't tell which patient population a model would be learning from.

“We analyzed opportunistic clinical recordings from 12 subjects (8 males, 4 females) during their clinical epilepsy monitoring.” · 10.1523/eneuro.0007-21.2021
Task design: only in the paper.

The paper says: naturalistic, unstructured everyday activities during clinical monitoring (eating, sleeping, watching TV, socializing). The data doesn't state it, so treat this as unconfirmed. Labels are hard to interpret without knowing what patients were doing.

“During clinical monitoring, subjects were ob- served during a variety of typical everyday activities, such as eating, sleeping, watching television, and social- izing while confined to a hospital bed.” · 10.1523/eneuro.0007-21.2021

How we checked

We read the dataset's descriptive files (sidecars, channel and electrode tables, NWB headers) without downloading the recordings, and the open-access paper(s) describing it (10.1523/eneuro.0007-21.2021, 10.1101/2021.07.26.453884). Values from a paper are only used when backed by a sentence quoted word for word from it, and they stay labelled as unconfirmed. We check that information is present and consistent; we don't judge the science. Paywalled papers are not read.
