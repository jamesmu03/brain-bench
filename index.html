4. What it asked

Only what it couldn't find in the files or the paper. In this test, another AI model played the researcher.

  • What licence should the dataset be released under? Your notes say "ask Nicole about the licence".CC0.
  • Dataset name and author list, in order?"sEEG Forced Two-Choice Task". Alexandra Rook, Bruno Stedman, Nicole Swan.
  • Power line frequency at the recording site, 50 or 60 Hz?60 Hz.
  • Where was the ground electrode placed?Don't know.
  • Electrode manufacturer?Don't know.
  • The paper reports four patients, but the folder only has PT01 and PT02. Should the other two be added?Don't know. Nicole would be the one to ask.

5. What it flagged for you to check

  • The paper reports four patients, but the folder only has two.
  • Many contact coordinates look wrong. Left-side contacts have positive X values, such as PT01 LA2 at X = 25.44, and contacts on one electrode jump around instead of following a line.
  • The ground electrode and electrode manufacturer are still unknown, so they're left as n/a.
  • Event times weren't checked against the photodiode signal.

  • Official BIDS validator: 0 errors (34 warnings, all about recommended fields left empty).
  • Our grade: A (0.94). Two checks fail: no DOI yet (you get one when you publish), and no electrode positions in a standard template space like MNI.
  • The grade checks that details are there, not that they're right. The coordinates flagged in step 5 still need a person to look at them.
  • Compared with the test folder's answer key: all 12 dataset fields, 84 channels, 192 events and 76 electrode positions match the lab's files, odd coordinates included. It flagged them and left them alone.
  • Patient details in the dataset or in anything sent to the AI: none.

Privacy

What leaves your computer

  • We find and hide patient names, record numbers and dates on your computer before any AI sees your data.
  • The AI provider gets the file names, notes, spreadsheets and recording headers, minus those details. The recordings themselves never leave your computer.
  • You'll be able to pick the AI provider and use your own API key.
  • While the AI works, the tool only talks to the AI provider, and it logs every request it sends.
  • There's no option yet to run the AI on your own computer.

Other tools

How it compares

  • Auto-BIDSify uses AI to convert MRI, fNIRS and EEG data to BIDS. ezBIDS walks you through converting MRI data. NeuroConv converts recordings to NWB.
  • MNE-BIDS can write iEEG to BIDS, but you write the script and track down the details yourself.
  • Ours is built for iEEG. It hides patient details before the AI sees anything. Then it asks what it couldn't find and tells you what looks wrong, like the demo's coordinates.
