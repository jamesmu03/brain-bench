brain-benchDataset grades

We graded every public intracranial EEG dataset

  • 77 datasets from OpenNeuro and DANDI.
  • For each one, we checked what important metadata was missing.
  • The grade is about how easy the data is to reuse, not how good the science is.

What's usually missing

Clinical / implant contextiFacts about each patient's condition and implant: diagnosis (e.g. epilepsy, Parkinson's), electrode type (grid, strip, depth), which side of the brain was covered, the brain target, and surgical outcome.Brain activity looks different depending on the disease and where the electrodes are. Without this you can't tell what population you're training on or control for it when pooling data from several hospitals.Examples: Diagnosis: drug-resistant epilepsy, Parkinson's disease, essential tremor. Implant: subdural grid, strip, depth (sEEG) electrodes, DBS lead, microwires. Coverage: left, right or both hemispheres. Outcome: Engel class after surgery.Passes when: The participants table has at least one clinical or implant column (hemisphere, implant, pathology, diagnosis, outcome, target...).56 of 73 datasets
77%
Amplifier model namediThe exact model of the amplifier that recorded the signal (e.g. Natus Quantum, Nihon Kohden JE-120).Each model has its own input filters, noise floor and digitization. A model trained on one amplifier can quietly perform worse on another, so you need to know which is which.Examples: Natus Quantum, Natus Xltek EMU128FS, Nihon Kohden JE-120A, Micromed SD LTM 64 Express, Blackrock NeuroPort, Neuralynx ATLAS, Tucker-Davis Technologies RZ2.Passes when: The model name is given in at least 95% of recordings.50 of 73 datasets
68%
Recording system namediThe company that made the recording system (e.g. Natus, Micromed, Blackrock, Neuralynx).The most basic fact needed to correct for hardware differences between hospitals. If it's missing, you can't even group recordings by device.Examples: Clinical systems: Natus (Xltek, Nicolet), Nihon Kohden, Micromed, Compumedics. Research systems: Blackrock Neurotech, Neuralynx, Tucker-Davis Technologies, g.tec. Implanted devices: NeuroPace RNS, Medtronic Percept.Passes when: Named (not blank or n/a) in at least 95% of recordings.42 of 73 datasets
57%
Ethics approval statediA statement that the data was collected under an approved ethics protocol (an IRB or ethics committee).This is patient brain data. Others need to know it was collected with consent and approval before they can responsibly reuse it.Examples: An IRB protocol number, or the name of the approving ethics committee, e.g. 'Medical Ethical Committee of UMC Utrecht'.Passes when: The dataset description lists an ethics approval.42 of 77 datasets
55%
Template (MNI) coordinatesiElectrode positions given in a shared reference brain such as MNI or fsaverage, rather than only in the patient's own brain scan.Every brain is a different shape, so raw coordinates from two patients can't be compared. A shared space is what lets you say two electrodes are in the same region, which is essential for combining patients.Examples: MNI152 (e.g. MNI152NLin2009cAsym), MNI305, fsaverage, Talairach. Patient-only spaces like ACPC or scanner coordinates don't count here.Passes when: Coordinates are available in an MNI-family or fsaverage space.28 of 53 datasets
53%
Reference scheme statediWhat each channel's voltage was measured against: another electrode, the average of all channels, or an electrode on the scalp or bone.The same brain activity looks different under different references. Recordings with different or unknown references aren't directly comparable.Examples: Common average of all channels, bipolar (each contact minus its neighbor), a scalp or mastoid electrode, an upside-down strip facing the skull, or a white-matter contact.Passes when: The reference scheme is stated in at least 95% of recordings.37 of 73 datasets
51%
